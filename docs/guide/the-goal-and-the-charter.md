The charter is the one document every project starts from: what it is for, how you would know it
is working, and what gets written down. You write the goal; an interview turns it into the rest;
you approve the whole thing once. "The gate is for comprehension, not correctness: nobody is being
asked to audit a schema, only whether this is what their project is for."

## In the console

The project-create form asks for your goal. The interview runs on the left of the onboarding
screen; the charter appears on the right, with an Approve button. → `#/guide/the-goal-and-the-charter`

## The smallest useful thing you can do

Type your goal as one sentence, an outcome not a category. Answer the interview's questions one at
a time. Read the charter end to end and press Approve.

## Ellen's example

When Ellen answers four questions, this is deposited, verbatim from the worked example. The
labelling rules go with it, but that is [the rulebook's](the-rulebook.md) page:

```charter
"goal": "Send one email newsletter every week to the shop's mailing list, about books we are excited about, so that people who have been in once come back.",
"measure": "In a month: four newsletters have gone out, one per week, and the subscriber count is higher than the 430 it is today. Ellen behind the counter should be able to say 'somebody mentioned the newsletter' at least once.",
"project_background": "An independent bookshop in Bristol with one shopfront and about 430 people on its mailing list. Ellen owns it and writes everything herself. Tone is personal and specific, never corporate."
```

(`go/orgprompts/interviewer.md`; the deposit also carries `label_rules`, `rationale` and
`architect_cron`)

## The trap

Depositing this changes nothing: no worker exists until Ellen presses Approve, which creates
exactly one worker, the architect, plus a schedule enabled by default. It never names who writes
the newsletter or any worker at all; deciding that is the architect's job. The charter is always
deposited whole; changing your mind about one sentence means writing the whole thing again.

## What this will not do

The interview will not design your team, only write down what it is for and how you would judge
it. It does not check that the charter is true, only that it is complete. It does not let you edit
one field of an approved charter, only write a new one.

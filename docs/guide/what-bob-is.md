If you have one task to finish today, use a chat assistant and stop there: one good model beats a
team of agents at a single task, reliably and for a fraction of the cost. Bob is for work that
keeps coming back. You give a project a goal, and a small team of AI workers forms around it, each
one a container, a prompt and a set of tools, woken by a clock or by something happening. Over
time the team edits itself, hiring new workers and rewriting prompts, and records why.

## The smallest useful thing you can do

Decide if you belong here. One task, stop and use a chat assistant. Work that repeats, create a
project and type what it is for in one sentence. That sentence starts an interview: see
[your first hour](your-first-hour.md).

## Ellen's example

Ellen runs the bookshop every page after this one follows:

> "An independent bookshop in Bristol with one shopfront and about 430 people on its mailing list.
> Ellen owns it and writes everything herself. Tone is personal and specific, never corporate."
> (`go/orgprompts/interviewer.md`)

## The trap

It sounds like a mesh of agents talking to each other. It is not: there is no message bus between
workers. A worker that wants to tell another worker something writes it down where that worker
will read it.

## What this will not do

It will not finish a single task faster than asking a model directly. It does not choose your team
for you, the architect does that, after you approve a goal (see
[the goal and the charter](the-goal-and-the-charter.md)). It is not a general chat bot: workers act
on a clock or an event, not on being typed at.

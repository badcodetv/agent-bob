The architect is the one worker in your project that isn't there to do the work itself. It designs who does the work, wires them to the clocks and events that wake them, and keeps changing that design as evidence comes in. Approving the charter creates just this one worker; it builds the rest.

## In the console

The architect shows up like any other worker, in Workers. Its changes appear on the Desk as rows you can open, each a rewrite with a reason attached. The toggle for its daily run is on its own Triggers tab.

## The smallest useful thing you can do

Open the architect's most recent note on the Desk and read what it changed, and why.

## Ellen's example

On its first run, before any team exists, the architect's own instructions tell it what it must build:

> You MUST include at least one worker subscribed to the event "worker.finished" whose job is to read what just finished and write summaries under the registry's labels.

For the bookshop that means at least one worker that writes the newsletter, and one that reads what it wrote and remembers whether it worked.

## The trap

The loop ends every changing run by telling you, through `request_human_attention`. A quiet architect is not a sign that nothing happened. It is the alarm. Three runs with no new evidence, and it says so itself; if it stops saying anything at all, act on that.

Read this in full, unsoftened:

> The architect's loop has no mechanical brake. Every rule in its prompt is an instruction it may choose to delete. Revert is the entire control, and the failure mode to watch for is the system quietly ceasing to tell you what it is doing.

## What this will not do

It will not ask before changing something; it acts, then tells you. It will not rewrite more than one worker's instructions per run. Freezing a worker is the only thing that protects it from a rewrite.
